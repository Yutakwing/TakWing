My journey

From movement to meaning

My work has moved from understanding how people move, to asking how future physiotherapists learn to think, decide, and care.

01

Why physiotherapy

Physiotherapy first attracted me because it joins scientific understanding with practical human judgement. Movement can be measured, but good care still depends on listening, reasoning, adapting, and seeing the person rather than only the condition.

02

Why education

Teaching made the hidden work of clinical reasoning impossible to ignore. Students did not only need more information; they needed opportunities to explain decisions, test assumptions, make mistakes safely, and connect theory with the person in front of them.

03

Why virtual reality

VR offered a way to make difficult, spatial, or rarely encountered experiences more available. My interest is not in immersion for its own sake. It is in whether a designed experience helps students notice, practise, and understand something that other methods cannot show as clearly.

04

Why artificial intelligence

AI raises a different challenge. It can expand feedback and generate possibilities, but it can also hide weak reasoning behind fluent answers. I am interested in designs that make students think before consulting AI, question what it produces, and remain accountable for the final judgement.

The problem I am trying to solve

How can we design learning experiences that make clinical reasoning visible, preserve meaningful human judgement, and use technology only where it adds genuine educational value?

Start with the learner

Define the competence, misconception, or barrier before choosing a tool.

Make thinking visible

Ask students to explain, compare, defend, and revise their decisions.

Design for transfer

Connect classroom activity to the uncertainty and responsibility of professional practice.

Evaluate honestly

Treat friction, failure, and unintended consequences as evidence for the next design.
