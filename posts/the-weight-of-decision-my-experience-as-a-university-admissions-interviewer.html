The Weight of Decision: My Experience as a University Admissions Interviewer

Last week marked a significant event in the lives of many young individuals in Hong Kong: the release of the Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education (DSE) results. This period is crucial as it determines the next steps in their academic journeys, and for some, it might dictate their future careers. As a lecturer at a university, I, along with other staff members, was responsible for interviewing students to assess their suitability for admission. It was a task that gave me an unexpected power – the power to influence a student’s future. This power left me grappling with a myriad of emotions and ethical considerations.

As I sat in the interview room, watching each hopeful student walk in with anticipation and nervousness etched on their faces, I couldn’t help but question my own qualifications for this role. Who am I to decide who gets in or not? This question lingered in my mind throughout the process. The weight of my decisions was overwhelming, knowing that my judgment could significantly impact a student’s career path and, ultimately, their life.

The gravity of the situation became more apparent with each interview. Each student had a story, a dream, and a unique set of circumstances that brought them to this pivotal moment. It was a humbling experience, realising that my role in this process was not just about evaluating academic achievements but also about understanding the person behind the scores. I felt a deep sense of responsibility to be fair and just, to look beyond the numbers and see the potential within each individual.

However, as the interviews progressed, I noticed the inherent subjectivity in the process. Despite attempts to standardise the evaluation criteria, the human element in the interview process meant that biases and personal perceptions inevitably played a role. Hearing some of my colleagues mention that a candidate’s appearance could influence their decision was disconcerting. The notion that someone’s physical attractiveness could impact their academic future was troubling. It raised questions about the system’s fairness and whether true talent might be overlooked due to superficial judgments.

This subjectivity brought to light the broader issue of assessing potential and merit. While academic results measure a student’s capabilities, they do not capture the full picture. The interviews were supposed to fill that gap, but the process seemed flawed. The idea that appearance could sway decisions highlighted the need for a more objective and equitable approach to admissions.

Reflecting on this experience, I realised the importance of advocating for a more holistic and unbiased evaluation system. As educators, we have a duty to recognise and nurture potential, irrespective of superficial factors. The power to influence a student’s future should be wielded with great care, ensuring that our decisions are based on genuine merit and potential rather than personal biases.

The emotional toll of this responsibility was significant. Each rejection felt like a failure, a missed opportunity to support a student’s dream. The faces of the disappointed students haunted me long after the interviews ended. I questioned whether I had made the right choices and was fair and just in my evaluations. The pressure to make the right decision was immense, knowing that my judgment could alter the course of a young person’s life.

This experience also underscored the importance of empathy and compassion in the admissions process. Getting caught up in the numbers and criteria is easy, but we must remember that behind every application is a person with hopes, dreams, and aspirations. By approaching the interviews with empathy, we can better understand the context of each student’s achievements and challenges, making more informed and considerate decisions.

In conclusion, my experience as a university admissions interviewer was a profound and eye-opening journey. It made me acutely aware of the power and responsibility that comes with the role. It highlighted the need for a fair and unbiased admissions process that recognises true potential beyond superficial factors. As educators, we must strive to ensure that our decisions are guided by principles of equity and compassion, understanding the profound impact they have on the lives of our students. This experience has shaped my perspective on admissions and reinforced my commitment to fostering an inclusive and supportive academic environment where every student can succeed based on their true potential. I wish those who got the post all the best for this program, and for those who did not get the opportunity, your path may lie elsewhere.

The experience of serving as an admissions interviewer underscores a crucial takeaway for educators: the need for a fair, empathetic, and holistic approach to evaluating prospective students. Here are key principles to handle such situations effectively: Recognize the Responsibility:

Understand the profound impact your decisions have on students’ futures. Approach each interview with the seriousness it deserves.

Strive for Objectivity: Be aware of inherent biases and actively work to minimize them. Rather than superficial factors, focus on the candidate’s potential, achievements, and character.

Promote Equity: Ensure every student is given a fair chance, regardless of appearance or background. Advocate for admission policies that emphasize a holistic evaluation of each applicant.

Embrace Empathy: Remember that behind each application is a person with unique aspirations and challenges. Approach each interview with compassion and understanding.

Reflect and Learn: Continuously reflect on your decisions and seek feedback. Use each experience as an opportunity to improve your assessment methods and contribute to a fairer admissions process.
