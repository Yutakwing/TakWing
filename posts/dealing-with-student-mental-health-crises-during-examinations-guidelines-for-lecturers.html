Dealing with Student Mental Health Crises During Examinations: Guidelines for Lecturers

Original WordPress post

Introduction

As educators, we’re not only tasked with imparting knowledge but also with supporting our students’ well-being. Recently, during a practice exam session, an incident occurred where a student experienced a significant mental health crisis, resulting in a two-hour delay and a challenging situation for all involved. This student did try to use this an emotional hostage to force the lecturer to pass “the student”. I feel we should respect the student, whether the student is trying to take advantage or not. Benefit of the doubt, the student had emotional and mental stress, and we should have empathy for them.

This blog aims to share insights and guidelines on how lecturers can effectively handle such situations to support the student in distress while maintaining a conducive exam environment.

Recognizing Signs of Distress

The first step in handling such crises is recognizing the signs of mental distress. Symptoms may include:

  • Unusual or erratic behavior
  • Inability to communicate coherently
  • Visible signs of panic or anxiety
  • Abrupt emotional responses

Immediate Steps to Take

  1. Ensure Safety: Ensure the safety of all students and staff in the room. This might involve calmly asking other students to leave the room if the situation escalates.
  2. Remain Calm: Your calm demeanor can have a soothing effect. Speak in a soft, gentle tone to reassure the student and those around.
  3. Provide Space: If possible, provide the student with some physical space. Crowding or too many interactions may increase their stress.
  4. Seek Professional Help: Contact campus security or health services for immediate assistance. They can help in managing the situation more professionally and provide the necessary medical or psychological aid.
  5. Postpone the Exam: Consider the wellbeing of the student as a priority. Depending on the severity of the incident, postponing or rescheduling the exam for the affected student may be necessary.

Communicating with the Student

  1. Show Empathy: Let the student know you are there to help, not to judge. Simple phrases like “I’m here to help,” or “I can see you’re really upset, let’s find a way to make you feel better,” can be comforting.
  2. Maintain Confidentiality: Respect the student’s privacy by not discussing the specifics of the incident with other students.

Follow-Up Actions

  1. Document the Incident: Record details of the event, including how it was handled and the outcome. This documentation can be crucial for follow-up actions and future reference.
  2. Support Services: Guide the student to appropriate university support services, such as counselling centers. Continuous support can help them manage their stress better in the future.
  3. Review Exam Policies: Reflect on the incident to see if there are ways to improve the handling of such situations in the future. This might involve setting up more robust support systems during exams or training staff on mental health first aid.

Conclusion

Handling a mental health crisis during an examination requires sensitivity, prompt action, and a focus on the well-being of all students. By following these guidelines, lecturers can help mitigate the impact of such crises and support their students’ mental health and academic success. Sharing these experiences and solutions increases awareness and preparedness, ultimately creating a more supportive educational environment.
