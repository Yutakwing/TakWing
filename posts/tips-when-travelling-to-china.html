Tips when travelling to China

Introduction

Ever since the 70’s China has opened it doors for foreigner to travel in and out of China, but as you may have heard, China is a developing country, which means there may her hurdles that one may need to overcome.

I have been in China myself for a new times, and over the course of these years, I have compiled a list of things at which I think will be helpful for those travelling to China for the first time.

Cash is King:

With the modern day technology, cash has been replaced with cards and applications, which is true in China as well. They use a system called WeChat Pay. South Africa once tried this initiative, but soon was dropped due to the decrease in popularity. Although the opposite is true in China. everyone there uses WeChat Pay.

Well, that’s contradictory to the heading yes?

That is because in order to use WeChat Pay in China, you first need the Chinese Currency, which is RMB (Ren Min Bi), AKA the Chinese Yuan. In addition to having the currency you also need to have a bank account with one of the local Chinese Banks. Meaning it’s a lot of effort for using WeChat Pay for the short duration that you’re going to be there. Hence, Cash is King.

Communication Platforms

It is important to note that China has banned google, you can read more about it here. But sadly, all google services are suspended. Meaning, Gmail, Google search, Google Drive and Play store. A good alternative would be creating an Outlook account, and forward all your emails to your outlook account so you can still access newly sent emails while you’re in China. If you don’t know how to forward your Gmails to your new email account, please have a look here.

In addition to google, other social media are also restricted, such as Facebook, Instagram, whats app. For other websites, have a look here.

With problems, there are always solutions. Microsoft’s Gurdeep Singh-Pall created something called the Virtual Private Network (VPN). VPN allows users to connect to networks around the world as if their device is connected or plugged into the hub. Below is a list of 2019 Free VPN applications that one can get before travelling to China.

  1. Hotspot Shield gives users 500 MB of data every day for free
  2. Windscribe gives free account holders 10 GB of data for free each month
  3. bVPN provides 30 minutes of free browsing per day
  4. hide.me offers 2 GB of data and 3 servers for free users
  5. TunnelBear offers GhostBear to help you bypass China’s firewall

Exchanging money:

Correct me if I am wrong, but once you have left the major city or airport, exchanging money is an issue.

Assuming you have travelled internationally, it would be best to grab some US Dollars (USD) with you before you arrive, that way it will be easier to exchange your USD into RMB at your arrival.

If you’re needing a guideline for exchanges and trends, XE is a good one to use. It takes the average of the buying and selling the currency. Of course, these numbers are optimistic, it will be difficult to get the exact rates displayed on XE.

Besides exchanging at the airport or Forex, you can also draw cash at the ATM, but that of course have costs involved, at which you may be charge from both your local bank and the Chinese Bank.

Time Difference

The time zone changes as you fly towards the Chinese Country. China is about six hours a head of time. Eg: If you’re in China 07h00, it would be South Africa’s 02h00.

Therefore, just be aware if someone does not reply you as you may be texting in the early morning.

Internet

Internet is freely accessible in China, but there is a requirement. You need a cellphone number. Most of the free WiFi in China requires you to enter your local Chinese CellPhone number. Therefore do not be surprised when they ask you to enter your cellphone number.

WiFi is a good limitless solution, but we also have 4G (or 5G coming soon). The two major cellular provider is: China Telecom and China Unicom. You may be able to purchase these SIM cards at the airport or at the corner shop. Do get someone who understands the bundles to go with you. Because there are a lot of bundles that you can purchase. Eg: Netflix bundle (in South African content) 20GB for 50 Yuan.

That is very cheap for watching series etc, but that bundle does not include everyday mobile data. IE: your WeChat app or whats app application will not run from that data, so you will have to buy additional data to use the social networking applications.

Adapters

Please have a look at the adapter used in China. It is a little different to other countries, therefore it would be advisable for you to bring along an adapter for you to charge and use your electronic devices.

Safety and scams

China is a very safe country. But caution should be taken where ever you go. Pick pocketing and scamming is common in China. If something is too good to be true, it probably is.

Walking around in China at night is fine, but still be alert and aware of your surrounding. Travel in groups, I guess the normal protocol should be observed.

Transportation

There are many ways of travelling in China.

  • Di Di Taxi (Uber)
  • Public bus
  • Tax
  • Walking
  • Public Bicycle

These way of transports can be used at your convenience, with a cost involved of course.

Chinese Currency

I think it might be important for you to be familiar with what the notes looks like from the Chinese Currency.

  • 100 yuan – Red
  • 50 yuan – Dark Green
  • 20 yuan – Brown
  • 10 yuan – Light blue
  • 5 yuan – Purple
  • 1 yuan – Light Green

Conclusion

Hope you enjoyed this little heads up blog. There are many things that one could consider as a hurdle, but I think it is also a good thing for you to experience it yourself.

Most of all, enjoy your trip!
