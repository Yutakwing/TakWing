China Hong Kongand Taiwan in South Africa

Original WordPress post

I should start with an introduction about myself, otherwise, this post would not make sense. My name is Tak Wing with a surname of YU. I was born in Hong Kong and moved to South Africa before 1997, and ever since, I have lived here as a South African.

I am not sure how clued up you are about the history of China and Hong Kong, but if you’re interested in reading up a little about it, you can find it here. All in all, Hong Kong became the Special Administrative Region of the People’s Republic of China. Meaning “一国两制” which means “One country, two systems”. If I were to explain that, pages here will not be enough, but in a nutshell, basically the government in China cannot have a say in what happens in Hong Kong, yet Hong Kong is part of China and is influenced by China. The topic I want to speak about is the differences in culture and also the hate or misleading thoughts of people from China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan.

Now first, you will have to understand that there is a “dislike” attitude and emotions of which the Taiwanese and Hong Kongers have against the Chinese (mainland). For example, if a Chinese (mainland) goes to Hong Kong for shopping, the attitude they receive will be different based on their ascent of the language, Mandarin. The Taiwanese and Hong Konger will get superb services, where the Chinese will get a poorer and somewhat negative attitude.

I have been in South Africa for nearly 21 years, during the first 17 years, I have refused and rejected any communication and contact with Chinese people. I will not step into a Chinese Resturant or shop unless it’s owned by Taiwanese or a Hong Konger. If you can understand that someone 11 230 km away from China can still have this stereotype and attitude towards the Chinese, image the magnitude in China and Hong Kong. I must confess that this attitude is not without reason. The Chinese (some) has really poor respect for people and the environment. This boils down the civilization. Some Chinese can really be uncivilized.

Consequently, there has always been a separation between the Chinese and the Hong Konger and Taiwanese. If I were to explain the relationship in an equation it would look something like this:

Yes, it is true the Chinese are isolated by the Hong Kongers and the Taiwanese. Furthermore, the opposite is true, the Hong Kongers and Taiwanese are closer. This was my exact thought during the 17 years of living in South Africa, I had mostly Taiwanese friends and even had a Taiwanese girlfriend. Fortunately, I have been given the opportunity to change and embrace my culture by experiencing and learning the way of Chinese. In 2015 I met people from China, yes, like before, I would refuse to communicate, but we played a similar sport, badminton. Over a period of time, I found myself befriending them. From this, I learned, dealt and embraced them into my life and I saw I was more Chinese than I was Taiwanese and Hong Kongese. I enjoy my time with my Chinese people and started visiting China every year, trying to explore a different kind of China. One of my hights was the Great Wall of China. I eventually felt that being Chinese and not having been to the Great Wall is an insult to myself as a Proudly Chinese.

It was sad because, from 2015, I lost most of my Taiwanese friends. We drifted further and further apart, resulting all my Asian friends being mostly Chinese. It was the sad truth, but I felt like this was not my fault, I feel the misleading information given from our previous generation was the issue. Now that I am in the Chinese circle, I feel it was unfair that the Hong Kongese and Taiwanese had such an attitude against the Chinese. At the end of the day, we are all Chinese. We should unite and not have internal separation.
