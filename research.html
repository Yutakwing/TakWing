Research

Research

My research sits at the intersection of physiotherapy, health professions education, learning design, and emerging technology. I am especially interested in how educational innovations can be aligned with curricula, implemented sustainably, assessed appropriately, and translated into meaningful student and professional learning.

Artificial Intelligence in Health Professions EducationResearch on the responsible use of artificial intelligence to support learning, teaching, assessment, feedback, clinical reasoning, and curriculum design, with particular relevance to physiotherapy education.
Virtual Reality and Immersive LearningDesign, implementation, and evaluation of virtual reality in health professions education, including curriculum alignment, learner preparation, accessibility, technical support, authenticity, cognitive load, and assessment.
Clinical Reasoning and AssessmentDevelopment and evaluation of learning activities and assessment approaches that make clinical reasoning visible, support feedback, and prepare students for safe physiotherapy practice.
Physiotherapy Education and Learning DesignStudent-centred teaching, constructive alignment, active learning, assessment design, educational technology, simulation, and the design of inclusive learning environments.
Movement, Rehabilitation, and Sports TechnologyEmerging work involving biomechanics, movement analysis, reaction training, rehabilitation, sports performance, and technology-supported assessment.

PublishedVirtual Reality in Health Professions Education: Qualitative Descriptive Study of Educators’ Perspectives

Tak Wing Yu, Michael Rowe, Jose Frantz. (2026). Virtual Reality in Health Professions Education: Qualitative Descriptive Study of Educators’ Perspectives. JMIR XR and Spatial Computing, 3: e52925. https://doi.org/10.2196/52925

This qualitative descriptive study examines how educators view immersive virtual reality in health professions education. It argues that implementation depends on curriculum alignment, scaffolding, and institutional support rather than hardware alone.

PublishedExercise therapy education enriched through interprofessional teaching

Tak Wing Yu, Ghaleelullah Achmat, Luzaan Kock, Gareth Smithdorf. (2021). Exercise therapy education enriched through interprofessional teaching. Medical Education, 55(11): 1308-1309. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14619

PublishedParticipation restrictions and vocational rehabilitation needs experienced by persons with a unilateral lower limb amputation in the Western Cape, South Africa

Tak Wing Yu, Liezel Ennion. (2019). Participation restrictions and vocational rehabilitation needs experienced by persons with a unilateral lower limb amputation in the Western Cape, South Africa. African Journal of Disability, 8. https://doi.org/10.4102/ajod.v8i0.456

This study examines participation restrictions and vocational-rehabilitation needs after unilateral lower-limb amputation in the Western Cape. It highlights major mobility barriers and argues for rehabilitation services that better support return to work and wider social participation.

Preprintvirtual reality, higher education, learning outcomes, technology, teaching and learning.

Tak Wing Yu, Michael Rowe, Jose Frantz. (2025). virtual reality, higher education, learning outcomes, technology, teaching and learning. EdArXiv. https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/wnx8b_v1

This preprint maps how virtual reality has been used in health professions education and where the evidence base remains thin. It highlights anatomy and clinical education as the main application areas, while also identifying questions of efficacy, learner experience, pedagogy, and cost.

PreprintThe Perception of Health Professions Educators in Using Immersive Virtual Reality (Preprint)

Tak Wing Yu, Michael Rowe, Jose Frantz. (2024). The Perception of Health Professions Educators in Using Immersive Virtual Reality (Preprint). JMIR Preprints. https://doi.org/10.2196/preprints.52925
