Research

Research

My research sits at the intersection of physiotherapy, health professions education, learning design, and emerging technology. It examines how educational innovations can be aligned with curricula, implemented sustainably, assessed appropriately, and translated into meaningful student and professional learning.

Signature work

Virtual Reality Acupuncture

Turning a spatial and safety-sensitive skill into a structured cycle of preparation, practice, and assessment.

The problem

Students need repeated opportunities to connect anatomy, point location, safety, and procedural reasoning, but access to guided practice is limited by time, place, and supervision.

Design response

A curriculum-aligned VR application with training, practice, and assessment modes, supported by preparation and debriefing rather than treated as a stand-alone simulation.

What learners practise

Spatial orientation, anatomical relationships, procedural sequence, safety decisions, and explanation of technique.

Current status

Current project: development and curriculum integration.

Next questionWhich parts of the learning experience are improved by immersion, and which still require direct teaching and physical practice?

Signature work

AI Literacy for Health Professionals

Helping learners verify, disclose, question, and remain accountable for AI-supported work.

The problem

Using a chatbot is not the same as understanding hallucination, bias, privacy, verification, or professional accountability.

Design response

A developing sequence of curriculum prompts, reflective writing, and a short interactive knowledge check that makes responsible-use decisions concrete.

What learners practise

Source verification, privacy judgement, bias awareness, disclosure, appropriate task selection, and responsibility for final decisions.

Current status

Active teaching resource and curriculum-development theme.

Next questionHow should practical AI literacy be assessed without reducing it to tool familiarity or policy recall?

Signature work

Clinical Reasoning Chatbot

Designing AI to ask better questions before it offers answers.

The problem

AI can produce plausible clinical answers before students have identified red flags, compared hypotheses, or justified a plan.

Design response

A pilot chatbot that prioritises safety screening, staged questioning, explanation, and reflection before suggestions are revealed.

What learners practise

Red-flag recognition, hypothesis generation, evidence use, prioritisation, explanation, and critical review of AI output.

Current status

Pilot study in development; no claims of effectiveness are made yet.

Next questionCan structured AI dialogue improve the visibility and quality of student reasoning without creating over-reliance?

Signature work

Teaching Human Physiology with VR

Using immersion to connect structure, function, movement, and clinical meaning.

The problem

Dynamic physiological relationships can remain abstract when students encounter them only as diagrams, slides, or isolated facts.

Design response

Short immersive encounters embedded within a wider lesson: preparation, guided observation, case application, peer explanation, and debriefing.

What learners practise

Three-dimensional relationships, structure-function connections, observation, explanation, and transfer to clinical cases.

Current status

Teaching approach under iterative development.

Next questionWhat is the smallest useful dose of immersion, and how should the surrounding lesson be designed to make it count?

Signature work

Simulation in Health Professional Education

Exploring how different simulation roles may direct attention towards different aspects of learning.

The problem

Simulation learning is often discussed mainly in relation to the student performing as the clinician, while less is known about what students may learn as peer patients, observers, or peer debriefers.

Design response

A structured role-rotation model involving Learner, Peer Patient, Observer, and Peer Debriefer roles, supported by role preparation, focused observation, guided reflection, and educator oversight for clinical accuracy and psychological safety.

What learners practise

Early student accounts suggest different emphases across roles: clinical integration, patient-centred awareness, analytical distance, and feedback dialogue. These are perceived learning possibilities rather than confirmed effects.

Current status

Ongoing analysis and manuscript development; effects on clinical competence and readiness have not been established.

Next questionDo role-specific learning experiences transfer to later simulation or clinical practice, and how can this be measured beyond self-report?
